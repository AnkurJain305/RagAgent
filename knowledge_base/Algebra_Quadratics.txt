# Algebra: Quadratic Equations and Functions

## Standard Form of a Quadratic Equation

A quadratic equation is an equation of the form:

$$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$

where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are constants and $a \neq 0$.

## Methods of Solving Quadratic Equations

### 1. Factorization Method

If the quadratic equation can be factored as $a(x - r)(x - s) = 0$, then the solutions are $x = r$ and $x = s$.

Steps:
1. Write the equation in standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$
2. Factor the left side as a product of two binomials
3. Set each factor equal to zero and solve for $x$

Example: Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$

Solution:
- Factoring: $x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$
- Setting each factor to zero: $x - 2 = 0$ or $x - 3 = 0$
- Solutions: $x = 2$ or $x = 3$

### 2. Completing the Square

Steps:
1. Write the equation in the form $x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x = -\frac{c}{a}$
2. Add $(\frac{b}{2a})^2$ to both sides
3. Express the left side as a perfect square trinomial
4. Solve for $x$

Example: Solve $x^2 - 6x + 8 = 0$

Solution:
- Rearranging: $x^2 - 6x = -8$
- Half of the coefficient of $x$ is $-3$, and $(-3)^2 = 9$
- Adding 9 to both sides: $x^2 - 6x + 9 = -8 + 9 = 1$
- Left side is now a perfect square: $(x - 3)^2 = 1$
- Taking square root: $x - 3 = \pm 1$
- Solutions: $x = 4$ or $x = 2$

### 3. Quadratic Formula

The solutions to $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are given by:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

Example: Solve $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$

Solution:
- Using the quadratic formula with $a = 2$, $b = -7$, and $c = 3$:
- $x = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{49 - 24}}{4} = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{25}}{4} = \frac{7 \pm 5}{4}$
- Solutions: $x = 3$ or $x = \frac{1}{2}$

## Discriminant and Nature of Roots

The discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ determines the nature of the roots:

- If $\Delta > 0$, the equation has two distinct real roots
- If $\Delta = 0$, the equation has one repeated real root (a double root)
- If $\Delta < 0$, the equation has two complex conjugate roots

## Properties of Quadratic Equations

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, then:

1. Sum of roots: $\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}$
2. Product of roots: $\alpha \cdot \beta = \frac{c}{a}$
3. $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta = \frac{b^2 - 2ac}{a^2}$
4. $\alpha^3 + \beta^3 = (\alpha + \beta)^3 - 3\alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta) = -\frac{b^3 - 3abc}{a^3}$

## Quadratic Functions and Their Graphs

A quadratic function is a function of the form $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ where $a \neq 0$.

### Properties of the Graph (Parabola)

1. The graph is a parabola
2. If $a > 0$, the parabola opens upward; if $a < 0$, it opens downward
3. The axis of symmetry is the vertical line $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$
4. The vertex (highest or lowest point) is at $(-\frac{b}{2a}, f(-\frac{b}{2a}))$
5. The y-intercept is at $(0, c)$
6. The x-intercepts (if they exist) are at the solutions to $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$

### Vertex Form

The quadratic function can be written in vertex form as:

$$f(x) = a(x - h)^2 + k$$

where $(h, k)$ is the vertex of the parabola.

To convert from standard form to vertex form:

$$f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c = a(x - \frac{b}{2a})^2 + (c - \frac{b^2}{4a})$$

## Common JEE Problems

### Problem 1: Finding a Quadratic Equation Given Its Roots

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the roots of a quadratic equation, then the equation is:

$$(x - \alpha)(x - \beta) = 0$$

Expanding: $x^2 - (\alpha + \beta)x + \alpha\beta = 0$

Example: Find the quadratic equation whose roots are 3 and -2.

Solution:
- Using the formula: $x^2 - (3 + (-2))x + 3 \cdot (-2) = 0$
- Simplifying: $x^2 - x - 6 = 0$

### Problem 2: Relationship Between Coefficients and Roots

Example: If one root of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ is twice the other, find the relationship between the coefficients.

Solution:
- Let the roots be $r$ and $2r$
- Using Vieta's formulas:
  - $r + 2r = -\frac{b}{a}$, so $3r = -\frac{b}{a}$
  - $r \cdot 2r = \frac{c}{a}$, so $2r^2 = \frac{c}{a}$
- From the first equation: $r = -\frac{b}{3a}$
- Substituting into the second equation: $2 \cdot (\frac{b}{3a})^2 = \frac{c}{a}$
- Simplifying: $\frac{2b^2}{9a^2} = \frac{c}{a}$
- Therefore: $2b^2 = 9ac$

### Problem 3: Maximum/Minimum Value of a Quadratic Function

The maximum/minimum value of $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ occurs at $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ and equals $f(-\frac{b}{2a}) = c - \frac{b^2}{4a}$.

Example: Find the minimum value of $f(x) = 2x^2 - 8x + 9$.

Solution:
- Here, $a = 2$, $b = -8$, and $c = 9$
- The minimum occurs at $x = -\frac{-8}{2 \cdot 2} = 2$
- The minimum value is $f(2) = 2(2)^2 - 8(2) + 9 = 8 - 16 + 9 = 1$

## Important Theorems and Results

### Descartes' Rule of Signs

For a polynomial with real coefficients:
- The number of positive real roots is either equal to the number of sign changes in the coefficients, or less than that by an even number
- The number of negative real roots is either equal to the number of sign changes in the polynomial with $x$ replaced by $-x$, or less than that by an even number

### Rational Root Theorem

If $\frac{p}{q}$ is a rational root of the polynomial equation $ax^n + bx^{n-1} + ... + k = 0$ with integer coefficients and $\gcd(p, q) = 1$, then $p$ divides the constant term $k$ and $q$ divides the leading coefficient $a$.

### Location of Roots

Cauchy's bound: If $x$ is a root of $ax^n + bx^{n-1} + ... + k = 0$, then $|x| < 1 + \max(|\frac{b}{a}|, |\frac{c}{a}|, ..., |\frac{k}{a}|)$.
