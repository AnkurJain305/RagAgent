# JEE Mathematics Sample Questions and Solutions

## Calculus

### Question 1

Find the derivative of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x - 1$ with respect to $x$.

**Solution:**

Using the power rule and the sum rule of differentiation:

$f'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(x^3) - \frac{d}{dx}(3x^2) + \frac{d}{dx}(2x) - \frac{d}{dx}(1)$

$f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x + 2 - 0$

$f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x + 2$

### Question 2

Evaluate $\int (2x + 3)^2 dx$.

**Solution:**

First, expand the integrand:

$(2x + 3)^2 = 4x^2 + 12x + 9$

Now integrate term by term:

$\int (2x + 3)^2 dx = \int (4x^2 + 12x + 9) dx$

$= \frac{4x^3}{3} + 6x^2 + 9x + C$

where $C$ is the constant of integration.

## Algebra

### Question 3

Solve the quadratic equation $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$.

**Solution:**

Using the quadratic formula:

$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$

where $a = 2$, $b = -7$, and $c = 3$.

$x = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{49 - 24}}{4} = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{25}}{4} = \frac{7 \pm 5}{4}$

$x = \frac{7 + 5}{4} = 3$ or $x = \frac{7 - 5}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$

Therefore, the solutions are $x = 3$ and $x = \frac{1}{2}$.

### Question 4

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the roots of the equation $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$, find the value of $\alpha^2 + \beta^2$.

**Solution:**

Using Vieta's formulas, we know that:

$\alpha + \beta = \frac{b}{a} = 5$

$\alpha \cdot \beta = \frac{c}{a} = 6$

Now, $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2(\alpha \cdot \beta) = 5^2 - 2 \cdot 6 = 25 - 12 = 13$

## Coordinate Geometry

### Question 5

Find the distance between the points $(3, 4, 5)$ and $(6, 8, 10)$.

**Solution:**

Using the distance formula in three-dimensional space:

$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2 + (z_2 - z_1)^2}$

$d = \sqrt{(6 - 3)^2 + (8 - 4)^2 + (10 - 5)^2}$

$d = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 25} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$

### Question 6

Find the equation of the circle passing through the points $(0, 0)$, $(1, 0)$, and $(0, 1)$.

**Solution:**

The general equation of a circle is $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$, where $(h, k)$ is the center and $r$ is the radius.

Let's find the center of the circle. The perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$ has the equation $x = \frac{1}{2}$.

The perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining $(0, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ has the equation $y = \frac{1}{2}$.

The center of the circle is at the intersection of these two perpendicular bisectors, which is $(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2})$.

The radius of the circle is the distance from the center to any of the three points, for example, $(0, 0)$:

$r = \sqrt{(\frac{1}{2} - 0)^2 + (\frac{1}{2} - 0)^2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

Therefore, the equation of the circle is:

$(x - \frac{1}{2})^2 + (y - \frac{1}{2})^2 = (\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})^2$

$(x - \frac{1}{2})^2 + (y - \frac{1}{2})^2 = \frac{1}{2}$

Expanding:

$x^2 - x + \frac{1}{4} + y^2 - y + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$

$x^2 + y^2 - x - y + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$

$x^2 + y^2 - x - y = 0$

## Trigonometry

### Question 7

Prove that $\sin(A + B) = \sin A \cos B + \cos A \sin B$.

**Solution:**

Let's use the complex number approach. We know that $e^{ix} = \cos x + i\sin x$ (Euler's formula).

Therefore:

$e^{i(A+B)} = \cos(A+B) + i\sin(A+B)$

Also, $e^{i(A+B)} = e^{iA} \cdot e^{iB} = (\cos A + i\sin A)(\cos B + i\sin B)$

$= \cos A \cos B + i\cos A \sin B + i\sin A \cos B + i^2\sin A \sin B$

$= \cos A \cos B + i\cos A \sin B + i\sin A \cos B - \sin A \sin B$

$= (\cos A \cos B - \sin A \sin B) + i(\cos A \sin B + \sin A \cos B)$

Comparing the imaginary parts of both expressions for $e^{i(A+B)}$:

$\sin(A+B) = \cos A \sin B + \sin A \cos B$

Thus, the identity is proved.

### Question 8

If $\sin \theta + \cos \theta = \sqrt{2}\cos(\theta - \frac{\pi}{4})$, find the value of $\sin \theta \cos \theta$.

**Solution:**

We know that $\sqrt{2}\cos(\theta - \frac{\pi}{4}) = \sqrt{2}(\cos \theta \cos \frac{\pi}{4} + \sin \theta \sin \frac{\pi}{4})$

$= \sqrt{2}(\cos \theta \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} + \sin \theta \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})$

$= \cos \theta + \sin \theta$

So, the given equation is an identity.

Now, we need to find $\sin \theta \cos \theta$.

We know that $(\sin \theta + \cos \theta)^2 = \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta + 2\sin \theta \cos \theta = 1 + 2\sin \theta \cos \theta$

From the given identity, $(\sin \theta + \cos \theta)^2 = (\sqrt{2}\cos(\theta - \frac{\pi}{4}))^2 = 2\cos^2(\theta - \frac{\pi}{4})$

Since $\cos^2(\theta - \frac{\pi}{4}) = \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta - \frac{\pi}{2})}{2} = \frac{1 + \sin(2\theta)}{2}$

Therefore, $(\sin \theta + \cos \theta)^2 = 2 \cdot \frac{1 + \sin(2\theta)}{2} = 1 + \sin(2\theta)$

Equating the two expressions for $(\sin \theta + \cos \theta)^2$:

$1 + 2\sin \theta \cos \theta = 1 + \sin(2\theta)$

Since $\sin(2\theta) = 2\sin \theta \cos \theta$, the equation is satisfied.

Therefore, $\sin \theta \cos \theta = \frac{\sin(2\theta)}{2}$

But we still need a specific value. Let's find it by using the given condition.

Squaring both sides of $\sin \theta + \cos \theta = \sqrt{2}$:

$(\sin \theta + \cos \theta)^2 = 2$

$\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta + 2\sin \theta \cos \theta = 2$

$1 + 2\sin \theta \cos \theta = 2$

$2\sin \theta \cos \theta = 1$

$\sin \theta \cos \theta = \frac{1}{2}$

## Vectors

### Question 9

If $\vec{a} = 2\vec{i} + 3\vec{j} - \vec{k}$ and $\vec{b} = \vec{i} - 2\vec{j} + 2\vec{k}$, find $\vec{a} \times \vec{b}$.

**Solution:**

Using the formula for the cross product:

$\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 1 & -2 & 2 \end{vmatrix}$

$= \vec{i} \begin{vmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -2 & 2 \end{vmatrix} - \vec{j} \begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} + \vec{k} \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & -2 \end{vmatrix}$

$= \vec{i}(3 \cdot 2 - (-1) \cdot (-2)) - \vec{j}(2 \cdot 2 - (-1) \cdot 1) + \vec{k}(2 \cdot (-2) - 3 \cdot 1)$

$= \vec{i}(6 - 2) - \vec{j}(4 - (-1)) + \vec{k}(-4 - 3)$

$= 4\vec{i} - 5\vec{j} - 7\vec{k}$

### Question 10

Find the area of the triangle with vertices at $(1, 2, 3)$, $(4, 5, 6)$, and $(7, 8, 9)$.

**Solution:**

Let's denote the vertices as $A(1, 2, 3)$, $B(4, 5, 6)$, and $C(7, 8, 9)$.

The area of the triangle can be calculated using the formula:

Area = $\frac{1}{2}|\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC}|$

$\vec{AB} = \vec{B} - \vec{A} = (4, 5, 6) - (1, 2, 3) = (3, 3, 3)$

$\vec{AC} = \vec{C} - \vec{A} = (7, 8, 9) - (1, 2, 3) = (6, 6, 6)$

$\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 3 & 3 & 3 \\ 6 & 6 & 6 \end{vmatrix}$

Notice that $\vec{AC} = 2\vec{AB}$, which means that the vectors are parallel. Therefore, their cross product is zero, and the area of the triangle is zero. This means that the three points are collinear (they lie on a straight line).

## Probability

### Question 11

A bag contains 5 red balls and 3 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. Find the probability that both balls are of the same color.

**Solution:**

Total number of ways to draw 2 balls from 8 balls = $\binom{8}{2} = \frac{8!}{2!(8-2)!} = \frac{8 \cdot 7}{2 \cdot 1} = 28$

Number of ways to draw 2 red balls = $\binom{5}{2} = \frac{5!}{2!(5-2)!} = \frac{5 \cdot 4}{2 \cdot 1} = 10$

Number of ways to draw 2 blue balls = $\binom{3}{2} = \frac{3!}{2!(3-2)!} = \frac{3 \cdot 2}{2 \cdot 1} = 3$

Number of ways to draw 2 balls of the same color = 10 + 3 = 13

Probability that both balls are of the same color = $\frac{13}{28}$

### Question 12

Three fair dice are rolled. Find the probability of getting a sum of 10.

**Solution:**

Total number of possible outcomes when rolling three dice = $6^3 = 216$

Let's count the number of ways to get a sum of 10:

1. (1, 3, 6): 6 permutations
2. (1, 4, 5): 6 permutations
3. (2, 2, 6): 3 permutations
4. (2, 3, 5): 6 permutations
5. (2, 4, 4): 3 permutations
6. (3, 3, 4): 3 permutations

Total number of favorable outcomes = 6 + 6 + 3 + 6 + 3 + 3 = 27

Probability of getting a sum of 10 = $\frac{27}{216} = \frac{1}{8}$

## Complex Numbers

### Question 13

If $z = \frac{1 + i}{1 - i}$, find the value of $|z|$.

**Solution:**

$z = \frac{1 + i}{1 - i} = \frac{(1 + i)(1 + i)}{(1 - i)(1 + i)} = \frac{1 + 2i + i^2}{1^2 - i^2} = \frac{1 + 2i - 1}{1 + 1} = \frac{2i}{2} = i$

Therefore, $|z| = |i| = 1$

### Question 14

Find all the values of $z$ satisfying $|z - 1| = |z - i|$.

**Solution:**

The set of points $z$ such that $|z - 1| = |z - i|$ represents the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining the points 1 and $i$ in the complex plane.

Let $z = x + yi$. Then:

$|z - 1| = |z - i|$

$|(x + yi) - 1| = |(x + yi) - i|$

$|x - 1 + yi| = |x + y - 1|$

$\sqrt{(x - 1)^2 + y^2} = \sqrt{x^2 + (y - 1)^2}$

Squaring both sides:

$(x - 1)^2 + y^2 = x^2 + (y - 1)^2$

$x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2 = x^2 + y^2 - 2y + 1$

$-2x = -2y$

$x = y$

Therefore, the locus of $z$ is the line $y = x$, which is the set of all points equidistant from the points 1 and $i$ in the complex plane.
